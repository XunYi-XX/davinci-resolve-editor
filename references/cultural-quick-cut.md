# Cultural-Event Quick Cut

For a longer emotional documentary recap, use [event-recap.md](event-recap.md).
For timeline measurement and version-comparison methods, use
[style-audit.md](style-audit.md).

Use this variant for a 30–90 second culture festival, exhibition, campus open
day, tourism event, or social-media recap. It is music-led and experience-led
rather than a complete record of the programme.

## Narrative Shape

A useful adaptable sequence is:

`scale/context → tactile detail → participation → cultural craft → performance
→ group farewell → hard finish`

1. Open with a wide aerial or space-establishing view and, if appropriate, one
   event-title overlay.
2. Move quickly into hands, props, decorations, food, paper, ink, instruments,
   or other tactile detail. In the first 5–10 seconds, let the viewer feel the
   event before explaining its schedule.
3. Show participation and interaction at stalls, workshops, or displays.
4. Introduce cultural expression through craft, writing, music, costume,
   dance, or performance.
5. Let the strongest stage or group performance carry the longest shots.
6. End with a rapid run of people waving, smiling, or looking to camera. Use a
   short black or graphic card rather than lingering on the last group photo.

## Shot Selection

- Prefer a complete readable gesture: a sticker being pressed, a brush
  reaching its stroke, an instrument being played, or a group beginning a wave.
- Alternate macro detail with wide geography. A fast cut stays legible when the
  viewer receives a new scale, subject, or action, not merely a new angle.
- Use close-ups of hands and materials for authenticity; use faces and group
  reactions for participation and emotion.
- Prefer many distinct source shots over repeated fragments of one source.
  Quick-cut coverage usually benefits more from breadth than long-source
  mining.
- Even short source clips may need further trimming. Select the action peak
  rather than using the whole camera take.
- Match repeated visual motifs such as festival colours, costumes, props, or
  waving gestures so rapid cuts retain a coherent identity.

## Rhythm

- A useful range is roughly 1.5–4 seconds for the main body, with short
  0.6–1.5 second bursts for final waves, reactions, or musical accents.
- Do not make the entire cut uniformly fast. Hold a performance wide, an
  instrument action, or a particularly legible cultural detail slightly longer.
- A verified 72-second cultural-event quick-cut contained 29 source shots with
  a 2.48-second mean and 2.24-second median. Its final 6.5-second group-wave
  sequence averaged about 1.3 seconds per shot. Treat these as calibration
  evidence, not quotas.
- Coordinate cuts with musical energy or phrase changes. Do not claim exact beat
  synchronization unless beat positions and cut distances have been measured.

## Speed and Transitions

- Use speed ramps primarily on stable aerial or spatial moves. In the verified
  reference, three aerial/stage shots used roughly 3×–8× compression.
- A single crash zoom can work as the opening transition from aerial geography
  into tactile detail. Do not add crash zooms at every section.
- A distinctive quick-cut pattern can combine many micro-dissolves with a few
  long blends:
  - 0.2–0.4 second dissolves smooth motion while still feeling like cuts;
  - 1.5–2.5 second dissolves may bridge continuous craft actions such as
    writing or paper-making;
  - default to hard cuts when the action cut is already clear.
- Distinguish transition objects from solid end cards. A 0.3–0.5 second black
  or graphic card can create an intentional stop after the final group wave.

## Opening Graphics and Sound

- A short full-frame title, title pan, or event graphic may sit on a higher
  video track over the aerial for roughly 2–4 seconds.
- Keep the event visual motif unified through source content when possible:
  repeated festival colours, stage banners, costumes, and signage can unify
  rapid cuts without applying a uniform filter.
- A continuous music bed often carries the quick cut. Add short risers only at
  a structural setup such as the title launch or first cut into detail.
- A five-second music fade can support the final wave montage and black card.
- If the intended form has no live-sound release, do not force location audio
  into the end; music-led social cuts may end directly.

## Comparing Quick-Cut Versions

When two timelines contain similar material, compare:

- track and clip enabled state;
- true last picture frame versus the timeline or audio tail;
- real-shot count and duration distribution;
- unique media and the one or two added/removed shots;
- source in/out changes, not only timeline duration;
- speed ratio and transition-type changes;
- which musical bed is enabled;
- final card and fade behavior.

A visually shorter revision should show where time was removed. In a verified
refinement, one low-information character cutaway was removed, a five-second
craft action was reduced to about two seconds, instrument and stage shots were
lengthened, and the ending gained a black card. This is a useful example of
deleting weak information while preserving performance breathing room.
