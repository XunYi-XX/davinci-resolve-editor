## Shared Editorial Principles

Give each shot a job: establish space, introduce a person, develop an action,
supply a reaction, reveal a detail, or provide event evidence. Sequence those
jobs into an information and emotional arc. Change shot length with pressure,
action, and sound rather than cutting every source to the same duration.

Use wide, medium, and close views to change what the viewer understands. Let
important gestures and results remain readable; use short shots for a specific
accent or accumulation. Repeated footage should contribute a new function.

Let action, eyeline, motion, or sound motivate joins. Use transitions when they
support a section, time change, softening, or intentional visual motif. Check
both picture and sound at the ending.

## Common Failure Modes

- Uniform shot lengths or continuous fast cutting without meaningful contrast.
- Attractive shots without a spatial, human, action, or emotional progression.
- Decorative transitions that obscure the decisive moment.
- Graphics covering the strongest human interaction.
- Music competing continuously with important speech or location sound.
- Treating source-frame samples as proof of current Fusion or graded output.
- Inferring instruments from filenames, or exact beat sync from tempo alone.

When learning from a reference, keep observation separate from interpretation
and recommendation. A request to learn remains read-only under the main skill.
